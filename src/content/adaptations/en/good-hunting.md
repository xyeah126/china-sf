Episode 8 of season 1 of Netflix's adult animation anthology Love, Death & Robots, released 15 March 2019, running roughly 17 minutes. Adapted from Ken Liu's short story of the same name, it is the only episode in the first season set in China and adapted from a story by a Chinese-diaspora author.

Directed by Oliver Thomas, adapted for the screen by Philip Gelatt, animated by Red Dog Culture House in South Korea.

The adaptation keeps the story's skeleton: the late-Qing father-and-son spirit hunters, Yan the fox-girl who loses the ability to shift, and the central premise that industrial civilisation has drained the world of numen. The emphasis falls on visual translation — steam locomotives, telegraph poles and the eventual cybernetic body are rendered as one continuous timeline of industrialisation, giving Yan's fall from spirit to woman and her rebuild from woman to machine a clear visual logic.

Against Liu's story, the animation reads the ending more decisively as rebirth: the mechanical body is presented as liberation rather than replacement, Yan recovers the power to run, and the frame brightens with it. The colder note in the original — that this is something gained only after something was taken — is softened on screen.

It is one of the few screen adaptations of overseas Chinese-language literature in this archive. Its existence is itself the point: the fusion of the Chinese zhiguai tradition with steampunk and cyberpunk is by now a proven proposition inside the English-language visual industry, and Ken Liu is the one who opened that path.
