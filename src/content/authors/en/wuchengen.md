Wu Cheng'en (c. 1500–1582) was a Ming dynasty novelist. Courtesy name Ruzhong, styled Sheyang Shanren, he came from Shanyang in Huai'an (today's Huai'an, Jiangsu). Son of a small merchant, he showed early brilliance but failed the examinations for years, belatedly earning the status of tribute student and serving around age sixty as assistant magistrate of Changxing, Zhejiang, before resigning and returning home. His poems survive in the collected works of the Sheyang Hermit; the hundred-chapter Journey to the West attributed to him tells how Sun Wukong, born from a stone, rebels against heaven, is pinned beneath Five Elements Mountain, then escorts the monk Tripitaka westward through eighty-one ordeals to the scriptures. The novel builds a complete cosmos of heavenly palaces, dragon courts, the underworld and countless gods and demons: seventy-two transformations, the cloud-somersault and the magic staff follow their own laws, and every immortal and fiend has a distinct temper. Fusing Buddhist lore, folk legend and marketplace humor, it is the summit of the gods-and-demons novel and an inexhaustible resource for all later fantasy, from classical wonders to contemporary science fiction and web fiction.
