Wang Jia (?–390) was an Eastern Jin occultist and writer. Courtesy name Zinian, he came from Anyang in Longxi (in today's Gansu). Reputedly abstemious, grain-free and prophetic, he lived as a hermit with many disciples; Fu Jian of the Former Qin repeatedly summoned him, and he was eventually put to death by Yao Chang of the Later Qin for his outspoken words. His Shiyiji (Gleanings of Prince Zinian) originally ran nineteen chapters and survives in ten, edited by Xiao Qi of the Liang dynasty. Spanning from the age of Paoxi down to the Eastern Jin, it threads together marvels of emperors and far kingdoms in gorgeous prose. Its account of a moon-piercing raft drifting on the western sea in the age of Yao, glowing at night and circling the world in twelve years, is often read as an ancient vision of visitors from beyond; elsewhere, envoys of the land of Yuanqu arrive at the court of the First Emperor in a snail boat that can dive beneath the sea, a kind of submarine avant la lettre. Few Chinese classics are richer in proto-technological fantasy.
