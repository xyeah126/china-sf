Li Ruzhen (c. 1763–1830) was a Qing dynasty novelist. Courtesy name Songshi, a native of Daxing (today's Beijing) who long resided in Haizhou, Jiangsu, he studied under the classicist Ling Tingkan and was learned in history, music, mathematics and medicine, yet failed the examinations repeatedly, held only minor posts and died poor but prolific. His life's work, the hundred-chapter Flowers in the Mirror (Jinghuayuan), was printed in the Jiaqing era. The first half follows Tang Ao sailing with his brother-in-law Lin Zhiyang through more than forty kingdoms: the Land of Gentlemen where all defer, the Land of Big Men where clouds grow beneath the feet, and the Land of Women, where Lin himself is seized and foot-bound, fantastic mirrors held up to real customs. The second half imagines Empress Wu opening the examinations to women: a hundred gifted ladies feast and display their skills in music, mathematics, medicine and riddles, a virtual encyclopedia of female talent. Lu Xun noted its discontent with social institutions and its habit of lodging ideals in episodes. In its utopian fable of reform and female brilliance, Flowers in the Mirror stands alone among classical Chinese novels.
