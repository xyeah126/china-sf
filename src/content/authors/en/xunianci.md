Xu Nianci (1875–1908) was a late-Qing novelist, translator and publisher. Courtesy name Yanshi, styled Juewo, pen name Donghai Juewo, he came from Changshu (Zhaowen), Jiangsu. A licentiate versed in English and mathematics and devoted to new learning, he founded the Xiaoshuolin publishing house in Shanghai with Zeng Pu and Ding Zuyin around 1904, later editing the monthly Fiction Grove (Xiaoshuolin) and becoming a central figure in the new-fiction publishing world. He himself translated scientific fiction from abroad and wrote the celebrated Mr. New Conch's Discourse (Xinfaluo Xiansheng Tan), in which the hero's soul leaves his body, climbs to the moon, plunges into the earth's core and roams underground wonders and alien civilizations—often cited as the finest original science fiction of the late Qing. Rarer still, he theorized the genre, arguing in essays on fiction, readership and knowledge that the novel should import science and reform society. Cut off by an early death, he sustained late-Qing science fiction almost single-handedly through writing, translation, editing and criticism alike.
