Gan Bao (?–336) was an Eastern Jin historian and writer. Courtesy name Lingsheng, he came from Xincai (in today's Henan). A diligent scholar summoned to the imperial library, he compiled the Annals of Jin in twenty chapters, praised as true history but long lost. Legend says that after witnessing the return of the dead within his own family he set out to collect tales of gods and the transformations of the spirit world, producing the Soushenji (In Search of the Supernatural), which he wrote, he declared, to prove that the way of spirits is not false. The original thirty chapters were lost after the Song dynasty; the received twenty-chapter text preserves some four hundred tales. Stories such as Gan Jiang and Moye, Han Ping and his wife, Li Ji Slays the Serpent and Dong Yong Meets the Weaver Girl already show fully formed plotting, an early form of the short story. Its motifs of metamorphosis, commerce with ghosts and rebirth supplied prototypes for all later supernatural fiction, making it a cornerstone of China's imaginative lineage beside the Classic of Mountains and Seas.
