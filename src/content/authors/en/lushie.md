Lu Shi'e (1878–1944) was a late-Qing and early-Republican novelist and practitioner of Chinese medicine. Named Shouxian, courtesy name Yunxiang, styled Shi'e, he came from Qingpu, Jiangsu (now part of Shanghai). Of a medical family, he practiced medicine in his early years, then supported himself by writing in Shanghai while continuing to see patients, producing more than a hundred novels across social, historical, martial-arts and medical genres. His Xin Zhongguo (New China) of 1910 is the masterpiece of his future-fiction: the protagonist dreams himself into a future China rejuvenated by constitutional reform, where Shanghai hosts a world exposition, trolleys and underground railways thread the city, and an iron bridge spans the Huangpu to open up Pudong, the concessions long since recovered and sovereignty in Chinese hands. Its visions of Pudong development, subways, river-spanning bridges and even a great exposition eerily anticipated the real Shanghai a century later, earning fame as a prophecy come true. With works like New Water Margin, Lu Shi'e was the great popular imaginer of China's future at the turn of the century.
