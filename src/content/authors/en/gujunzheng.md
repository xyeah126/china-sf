Gu Junzheng (1902–1980) was a popular-science writer, translator and publisher from Jiaxing, Zhejiang. Joining the Kaiming Bookstore as an editor in his early years, he long directed journals such as The Student (Zhongxuesheng) and New Youth Readers (Xinshaonian), devoting his life to popularizing science among young readers and translating many Western classics of the genre. When the War of Resistance broke out, he believed scientific fiction could rally morale and serve national defense, and between 1939 and 1940 he published three science-fiction stories in quick succession, The Dream of Peace (Heping de meng), The Strange Plague of London (Lundun qiyi) and Beneath the North Pole (Zai beiji dixia), later collected as The Dream of Peace. The first imagines an American scientist who invents hypnotic radio waves to turn the war by mental control; the second has an enemy attack London with artificially bred plague bacilli before being exposed; the third conceals a submarine base beneath the polar ice. All three anchor their speculation in the science of the day and press it into the service of the war, making Gu the representative author of Chinese defense science fiction. After 1949 he continued to lead popular-science publishing, a bridge between the Republican and new China eras of scientific outreach.
