Lao She (1899–1966), pen name of Shu Qingchun, courtesy name Sheyu, was a Manchu writer born in Beijing. After teaching in a primary school, he lectured in Chinese at the School of Oriental Studies, University of London, from 1924 to 1929, writing his early novels The Philosophy of Lao Zhang and Ma and Son; on returning to China he taught at Qilu and Shandong universities before devoting himself to writing. In 1932 the journal Xiandai serialized his satirical science fiction Cat Country (Maochengji): the narrator, with a companion, flies to Mars on an expedition, crashes, and awakens in the cat country, where he watches the cat-people, addicted to a narcotic leaf called Mistleaf, grow listless, cowardly, selfish and torn by factional strife. Possessing only the name of civilization, they collapse at a single blow when the dwarf-men invade and their whole nation is wiped out; the narrator at last returns to earth aboard a French aviator's plane. The novel trains its Martian allegory on the national character and the ills of the age, humor shading into cold despair. Lao She himself called it a failure; later readers have prized it as the earliest and most complete dystopian science-fiction novel in modern Chinese literature.
