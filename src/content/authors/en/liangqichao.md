Liang Qichao (1873–1929) was a modern Chinese thinker, statesman and scholar. Courtesy name Zhuoru, styled Rengong, from Xinhui, Guangdong. A disciple of Kang Youwei and partner in the Reform Movement of 1898, he fled to Japan after the coup, where he founded the journals Qingyi Bao and Xinmin Congbao and opened an age of enlightenment with his doctrine of the new citizen. In 1902 he launched New Fiction (Xin Xiaoshuo) in Yokohama, publishing in its first issue his political fantasy The Future of New China (Xin Zhongguo Weilaiji): the story imagines that fifty years after reform China has become a powerful constitutional state, and at its jubilee celebrations, with a world exposition opened in Shanghai, the old scholar Kong Juemin takes the stage to lecture on the sixty-year history of this New China, telling in the future-perfect the arduous road of constitutionalism, parliament, education and diplomacy. Only five chapters were written before he abandoned it, and Liang joked that the work was neither fiction nor treatise; yet it inaugurated the future-history genre in Chinese letters and directly fathered a line of utopian novels from Future World to New Era. It stands at the origin of China's tradition of political future-imagining.
