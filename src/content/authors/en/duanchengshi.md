Duan Chengshi (803–863) was a Tang dynasty man of letters. Courtesy name Kegu, he was the son of Grand Councillor Duan Wenchang. Entering office through family privilege, he served as collator in the imperial library and later as Junior Prefect of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices. Heir to a great library and a polymath versed in Buddhism, Daoism and technical lore, he wrote the Youyang zazu (Miscellaneous Morsels from Youyang), thirty chapters in the main and continuation collections, ranging over astronomy, exotic customs, flora and fauna and the secrets of the city, and famed above all for the strange; Lu Xun praised its extraordinary breadth. Its Tianzhi chapter records a hermit of Mount Song explaining that the moon is a body of seven treasures patched by moon-repairers, one of the most startling astronomical fancies in Tang literature. A virtual encyclopedia of the Tang tale, it is key material for tracing the deep roots of Chinese science fiction.
