Huangjiang Diaosou (The Old Angler of the Barren River) is the pen name of a late-Qing novelist whose true identity has never been established. In 1904 his novel A Novel of the Lunar Colony (Yueqiu zhimindi xiaoshuo) began serialization in the Commercial Press fortnightly Illustrated Fiction (Xiuxiang Xiaoshuo), running to thirty-two chapters without ever being completed. The story follows Long Menghua of Xiangxiang, Hunan, who flees overseas with his family after being caught up in a crime; when his wife falls ill on the voyage, a Japanese balloon maker named Tamataro and his wife come to the rescue. Tamataro dreams of reaching the moon in an improved balloon and planting a Chinese colony there. Long then travels with him by balloon through the South Seas, India and Africa, hunting a foe, practicing medicine and visiting overseas Chinese communities, the balloon serving as both vehicle and narrative hinge. To drive an entire long novel with a flying machine and lodge national dreams in exotic space was almost unprecedented in late-Qing fiction. Scholars generally regard the work as China's first original science-fiction novel: unfinished, yet marking the passage of late-Qing scientific fiction from the short story to the full-length form.
