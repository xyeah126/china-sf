Much of ancient Chinese imaginative literature grew out of collective oral transmission and centuries of accretion, its original authorship unknowable; with texts lost, pseudonymously attributed and recycled, the true hands behind many works can no longer be identified. This site uses Anonymous as a collective byline for such authorship, covering texts within the Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shanhaijing), the Soushenji, the Shiyiji, the Taiping Guangji and other early compilations whose authorship is missing or contested. These works span vast periods and mixed sources, yet together they form the distant source that precedes Chinese scientific imagination: in the forms of myth, natural lore, supernatural anecdote and the encyclopedic anthology, they preserve early visions of the structure of heaven and earth, peoples of far countries, flight and underwater travel, ingenious machines and the transformations of life and death, motifs that continually echo in later science fiction. Rather than invent an author for such texts, this site marks them Anonymous and lets readers confront the texts themselves, in keeping with its editorial principle of presenting documents as they are while treating questions of attribution with care.
