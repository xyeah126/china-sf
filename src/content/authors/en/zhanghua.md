Zhang Hua (232–300) was a Western Jin statesman, writer and naturalist. Courtesy name Maoxian, he came from Fangcheng in Fanyang (in today's Hebei). Of humble origins but vast learning, versed in charts, apocrypha and technical arts, he rose to Minister of Works and was enfeoffed as Duke of Zhuangwu, only to be executed for refusing to join Sima Lun's usurpation. His Bowuzhi (Records of Wide-Ranging Matters), said to have been ordered trimmed to ten chapters by Emperor Wu, catalogs mountains and rivers, distant peoples, rare creatures and occult arts. Its famous tale of the eighth-month raft, in which a man sails up the Milky Way to meet the Weaver Girl, is among antiquity's boldest visions of voyaging the heavens. Continuing the encyclopedic-marvellous tradition of the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the work fed Six Dynasties zhiguai tales and later encyclopedias, and remains essential to the early history of Chinese scientific imagining.
