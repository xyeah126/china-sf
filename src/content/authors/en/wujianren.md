Wu Woyao (1866–1910) was a late-Qing novelist. Named Wo Yao, with the courtesy name Xiaoyun and the pen name Wofoshanren, he came from Foshan in Nanhai, Guangdong. Born to an official family in decline, he went to Shanghai in his twenties to write for and edit newspapers, becoming one of the four great late-Qing novelists of social satire; his Bizarre Happenings Eyewitnessed over Twenty Decades exposes officialdom and society with a scalpel wit. In 1905 he began publishing The New Story of the Stone (Xinshitouji), forty chapters that bring Jia Baoyu back to earth in the Guangxu era. He first wanders the ravaged barbarous world that followed the Boxer catastrophe, then is guided by the Old Youth into the Civilized Realm, where he visits bone-verification institutes, electric trolleys, underwater hunt-boats and darting flying machines, and beholds a utopia that improves the race itself through science. Wrapping scientific nation-building in the shell of a classical sequel, the novel welds utopian future to technological marvel, the fullest marriage of old narrative and new imagining in late-Qing science fiction.
