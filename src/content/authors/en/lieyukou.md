Lie Yukou, traditionally placed in the early Warring States as a Daoist of the state of Zheng, is honored as Master Lie. Said to have studied under Hu Qiuzi Lin, he prized emptiness, lived in frugal contentment and declined office. The eight-chapter Liezi bears his name; the received text is generally held to have been compiled and embellished in the Wei-Jin period, though it preserves much early material. Its Tang Wen chapter is the strangest: the craftsman Yanshi builds an automaton dancer of leather, wood, glue and lacquer that sings and moves like a living being, often called the world's earliest robot; the physician Bian Que is said to have cured Duke Hu of Lu and Zhao Qiyin by swapping their hearts, drugging them into three days of coma and sewing them up anew, a startling anticipation of transplant surgery. Fables such as the Foolish Old Man Who Moved Mountains and the two boys debating the sun probe the limits of human effort, perception and knowledge. These speculations on machines, medicine and cosmos make the Liezi a rare specimen of technological imagination in early China.
