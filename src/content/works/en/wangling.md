A novel by Han Song published in 2019, concluding his medical trilogy. Taking death as its entry point, it asks after the relations between memory, existence and technological mediation.

It is set in a world where the dead persist as data. The departed are at once preserved consciousness and a new social stratum: they exist but have no rights, are kept but not heard. The boundary between the living and the dead becomes blurred and operable.

Its imagination continues Han's concern with institutions and the body, but pushes the critique to the ontological level: when persistence itself can be supplied by technology, what does it mean to be alive?

The most speculative volume of the trilogy. Filed as SF.

Of the medical trilogy this volume carries the critique furthest. The first two write of how institutions dispose of the living; this one of how they dispose of the dead — or even manufacture them. When persistence can be supplied by technique, death ceases to be a natural event and becomes a status that can be granted or withheld. Han thereby pushes the discussion of biopolitics to its logical end.
