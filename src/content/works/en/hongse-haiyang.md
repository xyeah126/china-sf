A novel by Han Song published in 2004, set after humanity has returned to live in the sea. It is his most ambitious and most systematic civilisational fable.

It rewrites human history across tens of thousands of years: the land is no longer habitable, humanity evolves, wars and migrates in the ocean, and at last returns to land. The book is built from chapters that stand apart yet answer one another, with crossed chronologies and shifting perspectives.

Its imagination treats the ocean as an experimental field in which civilisation is reassembled: bodily form, social organisation and ideas of life and death are all reset in that environment. The register is austere, the imagery dense, and the vision of civilisation's cycles pessimistic.

A core text for understanding the civilisational critique in Han Song. Filed as SF.

The novel is radical in structure: enormous leaps of time between chapters, constantly changing characters, and a reader obliged to assemble the outline of a civilisation for himself. Han thereby makes the unreliability of history into the form itself — since all records may be distorted, there is no need for narrative to supply the illusion of continuity. That self-awareness makes it one of the few experimental novels in Chinese science fiction.
