Zhang Ran's signature short story, published in 2012 and winner of the Chinese Science Fiction Galaxy Award and the Nebula Award. It takes the control of information as its central subject and represents his manner.

A technology called ether can filter and screen out particular information and sensations. People use it to avoid pain, anxiety and conflict, and lose in the process their capacity to judge what is real. The old physical term is given a new sense.

Its imagination turns information control from a question of political system into an implantable technical device. When filtering happens at the level of perception, resistance has nothing to take hold of.

Carefully written, joining technological imagination to human warmth, it is Zhang's best-known work. Filed as SF.

The story reads well alongside Folding Beijing: both treat the distribution of information, but Hao handles its segregation in space while Zhang handles its filtering at the level of perception. The first can still be crossed; the second leaves the filtered unaware of what they have missed. In the thoroughness of its critique Zhang's premise is the more unsettling of the two, and the closer to our present situation.
