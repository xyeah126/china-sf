A short story by Cheng Jingbo published around 2012, on symbiosis and the identity of the host. It uses a biological premise to probe the boundary of self and other.

A creature depends on a host to live, and the symbiosis alters the host's body and consciousness; whose body is being used by whom becomes a question without a simple answer.

Its imagination blurs the line between subject and object: symbiosis deprives the notion of a self of any clear extension, and ethical judgement becomes correspondingly complicated.

Brief, but joining biological premise to the question of identity very tightly. Filed as SF.

Cheng's short fiction often stops at an unresolved situation rather than offering a solution. The Host is a case in point: once symbiosis is established, asking who is whose host ceases to be meaningful. This relinquishing of certainty keeps her work at a distance from typical hard science fiction, yet brings it closer to the original motive of the genre — not to predict the future but to make one re-examine the boundaries taken for granted now.

For a short story, that is a remarkably complete statement.
