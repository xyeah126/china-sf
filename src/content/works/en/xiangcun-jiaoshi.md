A short story by Liu Cixin published in 2001, among his most emotionally forceful works. It takes rural education, a subject of the here and now, and lifts it to a cosmic scale.

Two threads run through it: a sick and impoverished village teacher insisting on holding class in his final hours, and a war between two civilisations deep in the galaxy whose outcome turns on tests of the civilisational level of certain planets. They converge at the end — the children's recitation of Newton's three laws becomes the credential by which human civilisation passes the test.

Its imagination lies in the juxtaposition of scale: the humblest village classroom and the grandest interstellar war are placed on the same narrative balance, and the meaning of education thereby acquires cosmic weight.

It was the inspiration for the film Crazy Alien. Filed as SF.

Its most affecting design is that the grandest judgement depends on the smallest act: the course of an interstellar war turns on whether a Chinese village teacher holds his last class. Liu lifts the meaning of education to a cosmic scale, and the answer is not abstract — the children have remembered Newton's three laws. Carrying a grand proposition on a concrete detail is the most effective technique in his short fiction.
