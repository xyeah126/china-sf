We Live in Nanjing is Tianrui Shuofu's third science fiction novel, serialised on Qidian in June 2021 and published in book form by CITIC Press in December 2022, about 400,000 characters in length. It stitches radio, apocalypse and one particular city together and is widely read as a landmark of online science fiction finding a "Chinese mode of expression."

In the story, Bai Yang, a Nanjing high-school senior in 2019, uses a set of ageing amateur radio equipment to contact, by chance, a girl named Banxia living at the same coordinates in 2040. By then the world has already fallen to an end-of-days disaster with no visible limit, and Banxia is the echo left by its survivors. Across twenty-one years the two trade weather, coordinates and ways to stay alive over the radio, trying to rewrite the ending together from opposite ends of time.

The novel is famous for its hard-core rigour: every space launch in the book corresponds down to the second with real launches of 2019-2021, and its technical detail on radio communication and space engineering withstands scrutiny. At the same time it is soft and luminous — the youthful correspondence across an apocalypse between Bai Yang and Banxia prompted Ma Boyong to praise it as "a union of tenderness and wonder."

It won the Galaxy Award for best online science fiction, the 2022 Xingyun Award gold prize for best novel and the Xingyun prize for best new writer, was named an Outstanding Work of the national Five-One Project, and was acquired by the British Library in 2024. Filed as SF.

Tianrui Shuofu once said that many classic SF concepts exist abroad but not in China, so he writes things bound tightly to Chinese ground and more Chinese in character. Placing an apocalypse between the traffic of Nanjing's Xinjiekou and a senior-year school desk is exactly that self-awareness made visible — cosmic catastrophe, for once, happening at the street corner you pass every day.
