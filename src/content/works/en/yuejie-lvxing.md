Lu Xun's translation of Jules Verne's De la Terre à la Lune, made from a Japanese version and published in Tokyo in 1903. It is among his earliest literary undertakings and a significant step in bringing Verne into Chinese.

Rendered in the chapter-verse style of the traditional novel, it abbreviates and domesticates the original to suit Late Qing reading habits — the customary method of the time, closer to adaptation than to literal translation.

Its lasting influence lies in the preface. There Lu Xun proposed that science fiction be warped with science and woven with human feeling, and argued that such writing could reform thinking and supplement civilisation, letting readers acquire the study of nature through pleasure. It is the first systematic account in Chinese of the function and value of science fiction, and is taken as the starting point of Chinese SF theory.

This entry records a translator's contribution rather than an original work, and is listed separately from Lu Xun's own fiction. In the same year he also translated Journey to the Centre of the Earth, further advancing the genre's circulation.

The preface weighs more than the translation. In it Lu Xun not only sets out the use of science fiction but draws its boundary: warped with science, woven with human feeling, either without the other and the point is lost. That formulation shaped the Chinese understanding of the genre for a long time, and its influence reaches as far as present-day arguments about hard and soft science fiction.
