The Stars is the comeback novel of Qi Yue after a decade away from writing, published in 2019 by People's Literature Publishing House. It tells a thoroughly hard-core science fiction story by the methods of a thriller, won the gold prize for best novel at the 11th Xingyun Awards, topped Douban's 2019 original science fiction list, and was announced for a Tencent series adaptation.

The novel runs on two tracks. One returns to 2025: Wang Haicheng, an associate professor of astronomy, receives a mysterious signal through FAST, the Five-hundred-metre Aperture Spherical radio Telescope in Guizhou, and is drawn step by step toward the truth of the universe — a discovery concerning the "constructs" and the dark membrane of the cosmos, large enough to put civilization at a fork. The other moves to 2030: after the German town of Glashütte vanishes without trace, Chengdu faces annihilation on the eve of a nineteen-nation summit, and two state-security operatives launch a twenty-four-hour counter-terror operation to protect more than ten million citizens — yet their quarry behaves nothing like terrorists.

The two threads meet over Chengdu: on one side people who look at the stars are bent by mortgages and academic titles; on the other, an entire city's fate hangs in the balance. Qi Yue deliberately gives his scientists a thoroughly worldly air, folding grand cosmic hypotheses back into everyday life — praised as a typical case of Chinese science fiction pursuing localised narrative after The Three-Body Problem.

The title pays homage to Alfred Bester's The Stars My Destination. Chengdu's landmarks and temper drive the plot; Wuhou Shrine, the Global Center and Sichuan University are all redefined by science fiction within the story. Filed as SF.

In his afterword Qi Yue writes that he set out to tell a story of people living in the gutter while still looking up at the stars. The sentence nearly sums up his relation to the book: a man who spent ten years in the games industry honing narrative craft returned to science fiction with not only technique but an obstinate wish to weld grand imagination back onto the ground of real life — and it is precisely this grounded looking-up that found The Stars readers among both science fiction fans and the general public.
