A novel by Hao Jingfang published in 2011 and an important starting point in her fiction. Its subject is the tension between the Martian and terrestrial branches of human civilisation.

Humanity has built an independent society on Mars; over generations the two worlds diverge deeply in institutions, ways of life and values. The protagonist travels between them and experiences the conflict and confusion that the division produces.

Its imagination lies in the divergent evolution of institutions: one human stock on two planets, constrained differently by environment, arrives at different forms of social organisation and in the end can barely understand itself. Hao's background in economics shows here.

Delicate in touch and strong on the relation between institutions and individuals, it prepared the ground for her later Folding Beijing. Filed as SF.

The divergence between Mars and Earth is not a contest of good and evil but a parting of paths under environmental constraint: with different resources, gravity and survival pressure, institutions naturally evolve in different directions. Hao thereby writes political disagreement as an almost natural process, which gives the book a tone closer to resignation than to indignation. Her training in economics shows clearly here.
