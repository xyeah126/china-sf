Written by Xu Nianci in 1905 under the name Donghai Juewo and published by the Forest of Fiction press. The title borrows from a Chinese translation of the German Münchhausen tales, but the content is entirely new, using only the frame for Xu's own design.

The narrator's soul leaves his body, ascends into the void, and visits the moon, Mercury, Venus and other bodies, observing their civilisations and cosmic marvels before returning to his flesh. Written in the first person in a singular register, it carries an evident intention of scientific enlightenment.

Two features of its imagination stand out. Treating the soul as an independently mobile entity circumvents the limits of contemporary aeronautics and makes interstellar travel possible — not unlike later fiction that achieves the same by uploading consciousness. And its descriptions of planetary conditions show a writer at home with the astronomy of his day.

Xu Nianci was an important Late Qing translator and editor who led the journal Forest of Fiction while writing and translating. Filed here as SF, it is among the most philosophical of Late Qing science fiction.

Achieving interstellar travel by the soul's departure from the body is an elegant conception: it circumvents the limits of contemporary aeronautics while giving the journey philosophical point. Xu thereby joins scientific enlightenment to metaphysics. Such writing was not unusual in the Late Qing, where the science novel often carried the double task of spreading new knowledge and discussing the nature of things.
