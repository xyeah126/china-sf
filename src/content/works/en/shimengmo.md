A short story by Cheng Jingbo published around 2011, using the legendary dream-eating tapir to treat memory and forgetting.

It is set in an age when dreams can be processed. The tapir feeds on dreams, and dreams are at once the residue of memory and the vehicle of desire and trauma. When a dream can be taken away, what exactly does a person lose?

Its imagination rewrites forgetting from a natural process into a technical service. The story keeps Cheng's customary restraint, handling feeling implicitly rather than sentimentally.

Among her best short pieces, showing her ability to keep emotion true inside a fantastic frame. Filed as SF.

Processing is a keyword in Cheng's work: whether sky, dream or symbiosis, what interests her is something operable that ought not to be operated on. This lends her fiction a faint sorrow — not because technology brings catastrophe, but because it makes what should be endured optional. That delicacy of proportion is the quality hardest to replace in her.

This tendency also explains why her work is often placed with the more literary strand of Chinese science fiction.
