A short-story collection published by Hao Jingfang in 2016, including Folding Beijing along with other pieces. Issued in the year she won the Hugo, it is an important text for understanding the whole of her work.

The pieces vary in subject but share a concern with lonely individuals inside technological society: some seek substitute feeling in virtual worlds, some lose their place in a hyper-specialised order, some face inward predicaments no technology can resolve.

Its imagination turns inward. Unlike Liu Cixin's cosmic scale or Han Song's institutional absurdity, Hao attends to the psychological situation of the individual within a technological environment, delicate and restrained, rarely resorting to grand spectacle.

The collection shows the variety of subject and temper in Chinese science fiction. Filed as SF.

The title of the collection is itself a judgement: loneliness is not an occasional state of individuals but the underlying structure of a technological society. In Hao's fiction the loneliness is rarely a lack of company but a lack of any possibility of being understood, since each person is enclosed in his own data. This makes her science fiction distinctly contemporary, and interestingly complementary to Liu Cixin's cosmic scale.
