A novel by Liu Cixin published in 2003, built on an extreme premise: high-energy radiation from a supernova will kill everyone on Earth over the age of thirteen within a short time, and the world is handed entirely to children.

The first half follows the adults as they pass on as much knowledge and as many institutions as they can in the time left; the second follows the children once they inherit the world — carnival first, then chaos and war, and at last a new order.

Its imagination is a thoroughgoing social experiment: by removing every adult, the novel can ask which parts of human society are learned and which come with age. Liu extrapolates several social forms, in an austere register.

The most extreme in premise of his novels. Filed as SF.

Liu shows here an interest rare in him, almost sociological: rather than concentrating on a few heroes, he devotes much of the book to the succession of social forms. The children move from imitating adults to discarding them to inventing their own rules, a process rendered both absurd and logical. His treatment of toys, games and their relation to war is especially austere.
