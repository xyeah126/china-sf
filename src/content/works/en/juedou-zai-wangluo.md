Xinghe's breakthrough work, published in 1996 and among the earliest Chinese science fiction stories built around computer networks; the internet had only just reached China, and the story appeared almost simultaneously with the technology.

It follows an unseen contest in network space: the protagonist meets an adversary of unknown identity, the two tracking, disguising themselves and attacking one another by technical means, with identity itself becoming the central riddle.

Its imagination gathers in two places. The network is written as a place with spatial quality that can be entered, rare in Chinese science fiction before this; and virtual identity is interrogated — when both sides may wear masks, who is duelling whom.

It marks the direct response of 1990s Chinese science fiction to new technology and established Xinghe among the most important younger SF writers of the day. Filed as SF.

The timing is worth noting: in 1996 internet users in China were still very few. Xinghe wrote it almost from concept rather than experience, which lends the story a peculiar prophetic quality — it assumes both the spread of the network and the anxiety about identity that follows. Reread two decades later, its judgement that virtual identity is unreliable has been confirmed by ordinary life.
