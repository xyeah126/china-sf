Full Spectrum Barrage Jamming is a novella of military science fiction that Liu Cixin published in 2001, winner of the 13th Chinese Galaxy Award for 2001. A second version of the story sets the action in China; the two versions share the same core plot and differ only in nationality and characters.

The premise is a full-scale war in which a US-led NATO alliance attacks Russia: the Russian army, hopelessly outmatched in electronic warfare, falls back battle after battle. Its only chance is to blanket the entire spectrum with electromagnetic jamming, dragging both sides back to a form of war from before the electronic age. At the critical moment the astrophysicist Misha, the lone occupant of a space complex, steers his vessel into the sun; the precisely aimed collision triggers a magnetic storm that cuts off communications worldwide — and humanity must for the first time fight a modern war by the most primitive means.

The novella treats dominance of the electromagnetic spectrum as the decisive variable of war: when every electronic-dependent combat system fails at once, victory is decided again between tank columns and infantry will. Its critique of technological dependence and its sense of civilizational fragility are widely read as an early warning of the technological pessimism of The Three-Body Problem.

A representative work of Liu Cixin's military science fiction and an essential text for tracing his thinking; a film adaptation was filed for approval in 2020. Filed as SF.

Notable is the story's convergence on a "shared dark battlefield": after technology annihilates itself symmetrically, war returns to an older and fairer form of confrontation. This instinct — that a weaker civilization might counter a stronger one by asymmetric means — carries directly into The Three-Body Problem.
