A short-story collection by Baoshu published around 2015, on the long-term fate of terrestrial civilisation; the title itself carries an elegiac tone.

The pieces span immense stretches of time: from the first rise of civilisation to its ending, from the fortunes of different species to the fate of the universe itself. Several answer to the cosmology of the Three-Body trilogy.

Its imagination is marked by the long duration. With philosophical training as well as novelistic skill, Baoshu paces narrative across vast scales, giving the rise and fall of civilisations a watchable shape.

It shows his command of the short form. Filed as SF.

Baoshu's short fiction often carries an elegiac temper: he places civilisation at a point already ended or about to end, and looks back from there. The perspective owes something to his philosophical training — what concerns him is not how events occur but what they mean seen from the end. Several pieces in The Ancient Song of Earth are almost funerary addresses written at the scale of cosmic history.

The pieces are better read together: individually their force is limited, but seen as a whole they form a single consistent voice.
