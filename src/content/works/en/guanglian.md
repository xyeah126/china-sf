A representative short story by He Xi published around 1999, building a love story on the physical properties of light. He Xi began publishing in the late 1990s, and this piece helped establish his manner.

A researcher whose work is bound up with light finds his emotional life entangled with his understanding of it: light takes time to travel, so all that is seen is past; the same unremovable delay governs understanding between people.

Its imagination turns a physical fact into an emotional metaphor that holds strictly — the finite speed of light is at once a scientific fact and an exact figure for the characters' relation. This method, in which the premise is the theme, distinguishes He Xi from other writers.

It set the course of his work, weighing science and the humanities equally. Filed as SF.

Most of He Xi's early work takes a physical concept as its subject while landing on human feeling. What makes Love of Light moving is that it does not set the coldness of science against the warmth of emotion; one and the same principle explains both. This became a structure he returned to: establish a strict natural law, then let his characters live inside it without escape.
