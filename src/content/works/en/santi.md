The first volume of Liu Cixin's Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy, serialised in Science Fiction World from 2006 and published in book form by Chongqing Publishing House in 2008. Translated by Ken Liu, it won the 2015 Hugo Award for Best Novel, the first Asian novel to receive the prize.

Two timelines interweave. In one, during the Cultural Revolution, the secret Red Coast project sends a signal into the sun, and Ye Wenjie receives a warning eight years later from the Trisolaran world four light-years away. In the other, set in the present, scientists die in succession and the nanomaterials expert Wang Miao uncovers the nature of Trisolaran civilisation through a game.

Its imagination rests on two premises. The three-body problem — the irregular motion of three suns — makes the Trisolaran world unpredictable, so their civilisation is destroyed and reborn repeatedly. And the two axioms of cosmic sociology yield the book's central dark forest rule. The archer and farmer hypotheses and the Operation Guzheng sequence have become shared memories of Chinese science fiction.

Fusing hard physics, historical reflection and the fate of civilisations, it marks the turning point at which Chinese science fiction became international. Filed as SF, with links to six screen adaptations.
