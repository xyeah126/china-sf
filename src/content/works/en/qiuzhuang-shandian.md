A novel by Liu Cixin published in 2004, taking as its premise ball lightning, a natural phenomenon still not fully explained. It shares part of its world with the Three-Body trilogy and is a representative work of Liu's hard science fiction.

Having watched ball lightning kill his parents as a boy, the protagonist spends his life pursuing its nature. The research leads at last to the strange character of the quantum world — the relations between observer, probability and reality. The novel also imagines weapons built on this principle and their military consequences.

Its imagination concentrates on narrativising quantum mechanics. Liu transposes abstract principles such as observation altering state into concrete plot mechanisms, so that hard physics directly drives human fate.

An important text for understanding Liu's method in hard science fiction. Filed as SF.

Ball lightning remains not fully explained, which makes it ideal material for science fiction: real observation to build on, ample theoretical room to move. Liu's method is to respect the phenomenon as real and then lead it step by step toward the quantum level. This path from the actual into the speculative is what distinguishes him from other hard-SF writers — he rarely invents a premise from nothing, but always starts from what is known.
