The concluding volume of the Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy, published in 2010. It pushes both scale and ending to the limit and is among the most ambitious works in Chinese science fiction.

After the Deterrence Era ends, the Trisolaran world is destroyed, and the solar system, its coordinates exposed, faces a strike from a higher civilisation. Humanity tries several escape plans — the bunker project, lightspeed ships, the black domain — while the ending points to a truth far larger than expected: the universe was originally ten-dimensional and has been reduced by war to three.

Its imagination centres on dimensionality: the dimensional strike turns a physical concept into a weapon, and the scene in which a dual-vector foil flattens the solar system is among the most famous in Chinese science fiction. The book also handles lightspeed flight, timelines and the rebooting of the cosmos.

It lifts the whole series into an epic of civilisation on a cosmic scale. Filed as SF.

The character of Cheng Xin has proved hugely controversial: every choice she makes comes from goodwill and leads to a worse outcome. Liu thereby makes the failure of morality under extreme conditions the sharpest element of the book, and the argument over its ending continues to this day.
