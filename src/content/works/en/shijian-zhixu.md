A novel by Baoshu published in 2013, built on a time loop and an ambitious work of philosophical science fiction.

From a certain day the world falls into an endless repetition of that day: everything resets each dawn, and only human memory persists. After the first chaos, society reorganises around the new normal, and religion, power and science each explain and exploit the loop in their own way.

Its imagination treats the loop as the condition of a civilisational experiment: when death is no longer final and accumulation loses meaning, how will humanity redefine value? The novel extrapolates the history of the loop over decades, centuries and beyond.

Baoshu's signature work and the most systematic Chinese novel on the time loop. Filed as SF.

The central difficulty of the time-loop subject is keeping repetition from becoming monotonous. Baoshu's solution is to let society keep evolving through the loops: as memory accumulates, power, religion and science each develop ways of coping with the cycle. What repeats is not events but the relation between events and response. This allows the novel to sustain change across a great length, and distinguishes it from other works in the mode.
