The Red Stone, published in 2022 as part of the "Cornerstone of Chinese Science Fiction" series, is a full-length novel by Jiang Bo. To merchants it is a priceless treasure; to believers it is a sacred object — the regular icosahedral crystal that has drifted to the remote planet Higgs is the key left behind by a lost civilization, and its appearance draws interstellar powers one after another, igniting both war and redemption. It won the Silver Prize for 2022 Best Novel at the 14th Xingyun Awards.

Matt and Randis were both born in the crowded underground city and grew up together, yet their fates diverge completely. When Matt comes upon the Red Stone by chance, the two are pulled onto a chessboard that spans the galaxy: believers who regard him as their hope, gangsters who treat him as prey, and civilizations moving pieces far above them. In twin narrative threads Jiang Bo tells a story of being swept along into adulthood — how two boys from the underground make their choices in the currents of the stars.

The novel keeps the space-epic temper Jiang Bo is known for, but holds the lens closer to the ground: instead of unfolding the rise and fall of civilizations directly, he looks up at the sea of stars from the vantage of one stone, two people and an underground city. The double edge of technology is as present as ever — the Red Stone can ignite war or save lives; what matters is the hand that holds it.

As the publisher's introduction puts it, Jiang Bo's characters always carry a certain youthfulness — fearless, resolute, and ready to fold personal fate into the tide of their era. That fits The Red Stone precisely: it is a novel about growth and choice, except that its growth happens at the edge of a planet, and its choices bear on the future of a civilization. Filed as SF.
