A novel published by Wang Jinkang in 2013, on humanity's flight and choices before a catastrophe on a cosmic scale. It is among his largest works and hardest in cosmological premise.

The universe itself is undergoing some fundamental change, and physical law is failing. Humanity must understand the change within a narrowing window and find a way out of the mother universe. Several character lines run in parallel, covering scientists, ordinary families and decision-makers.

Its imagination rests on contemporary cosmology: frontier hypotheses such as vacuum decay and the variation of physical constants are used directly as plot foundations. Compared with the solar crisis of The Wandering Earth, the threat here is more radical — not that the home becomes uninhabitable, but that law itself will change.

An apocalyptic narrative on a grand scale and a representative work of Wang's later hard science fiction. Filed as SF.

The question it poses is radical: if the threat comes not from an enemy nor from exhausted resources but from a change in physical law itself, what can flight mean? Wang's answer is that what humanity can rely on remains the speed of understanding and of organisation. This placing of knowledge above technique runs through all his work, and explains why it retains an engineer's optimism.
