The first volume of Jiang Bo's Heart of the Galaxy trilogy, published in 2012. The series is the representative work of contemporary Chinese space opera, supporting a grand structure with hard technical premises.

It is set millennia after humanity has spread through the galaxy: a loose commonwealth of civilisations spans the star regions, while a threat of unknown origin gathers at the galactic edge. The protagonist is drawn in and approaches the secret at the galaxy's heart.

Its imagination joins scale to precision. The spatial scope runs to tens of thousands of light-years, yet ship construction, weapon principles and the time cost of interstellar travel are all given concrete technical account, avoiding the vagueness to which space opera is prone.

It laid the foundation for the series, whose later volumes extend its cosmology. Filed as SF.

Space opera has long been marginal in Chinese science fiction, partly because it demands both vast scale and technical credibility, which tend to pull against each other. Jiang's answer is to trade pace for density: he does not shy from concrete accounts of ships, weapons and travel times, even at the cost of narrative speed. This trade makes Heart of the Galaxy unusually solid among Chinese space fiction.
