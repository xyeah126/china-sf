Strange Tales of a Small Town, published in 2021, is Qi Yue's fourth novel, released by People's Literature Publishing House. It returns to Hanwang, the small Sichuan town where he grew up — a mountain industrial town that rose with the "Third Front" construction of the 1960s and fell quiet as that era receded — and won the Silver Prize for 2021 Best Novel at the 13th Xingyun Awards.

On May 4, 1999, four teenagers discover, through the sudden disappearance of a companion, that something is wrong with their town — a place still guarding a core secret of the Republic. The residents go on welcoming the coming millennium unaware, but the world's timeline has stalled; countless possibilities are tangling together, and a great detonation capable of ending the town, humanity and even the universe is closing in. The mysterious transparent creature keeps its silence — and the teenagers must restart civilization before everything ends.

Unlike the urban hard-SF of The Stars, this novel sets a cosmic premise inside a Chinese township of the 1990s: the state factory before its restructuring, video parlours and old workshops, the loyalty and secrets of boys on the verge of youth. Writing it was, in a sense, a love letter to his hometown and his boyhood — the jacket line "with The Stars he saved Chengdu; with Strange Tales of a Small Town he saved himself" points to exactly that private origin.

Pan Haitian writes in his preface that beyond the leap from a sealed small town to the foundational level of the cosmos, what moves the reader more are the typically Chinese anxieties, emotions and angers in the story. That places Qi Yue precisely: he does not rehearse the cliché of boys saving the world, but sinks the secret of the universe into the soil of one particular town, and lets the boys dig it out. Filed as SF.
