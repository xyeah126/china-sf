An oceanic science fiction novel by Zheng Wenguang published around 1979. In Zheng's work, space and the sea form two parallel lines of exploration; this book belongs to the latter.

A deep-sea expedition on an ocean mission meets a series of anomalies and organisms in extreme conditions and at last makes an important discovery. The narrative continues the author's customary manner, supporting the course of exploration with concrete technical detail.

Its imagination concentrates on the deep sea as a special space: high pressure, no light, unfamiliar life forms, already a naturally heterogeneous world. Its descriptions of submersible equipment, lighting and communication show a writer at home with marine science.

Part of the widening of his subject matter, it also reflects the interest of Chinese science fiction in the late 1970s in varied spaces of exploration. Filed as SF.

In Chinese science fiction of the late 1970s the sea stood alongside space as the other great field of exploration. Zheng wrote on both lines, giving his work a symmetry: the starry sky above, the deep sea below, each requiring technology to enter a realm humans cannot inhabit unaided. In the Depths of the Ocean belongs to the second, and shows his consistent practice of writing scientific procedure into narrative.
