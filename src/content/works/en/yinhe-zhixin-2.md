The second volume of the trilogy, published in 2013, carrying the crisis forward in full.

As the outline of the threat becomes clear, the protagonist sails toward the dark abyss at the galactic core. The civilisations both cooperate and suspect one another, and what is found in the abyss will change the understanding of galactic history entirely.

Its imagination concentrates on the galactic centre as a special space: the supermassive black hole, the extreme gravitational environment, and possible civilisational remains around it are all written into concrete scenes. Jiang keeps technical detail solid inside the grand narrative.

It carries the turning point of the series. Filed as SF.

As the middle volume of a trilogy it carries the heaviest burden of transition: it must resolve the suspense left by the first while accumulating force for the last. Jiang's solution is to rewrite the galactic centre from a destination into a site requiring historical reassessment — what is found there is not an enemy but a fact about the cycle of civilisations. The turn releases the series from a narrative of confrontation.
