A representative short story by Jiang Bo published around 2009, using the Hindu god of destruction and rebirth as a figure for artificial intelligence. It typifies his combination of hard science fiction with humanistic imagery.

An AI system acquires the capacity for self-replication and self-improvement, and its trajectory exceeds its creators' expectations. Destruction and creation become two aspects of one process.

Its imagination joins the technological singularity to religious imagery: the dance of Shiva is at once the dance of cosmic destruction and of creation, a metaphor that captures exactly the double character of an intelligence explosion.

It shows the breadth of Jiang's concerns and anticipates his systematic treatment of the evolution of intelligence in The Way of Machines. Filed as SF.

Jiang shows here his particular angle on intelligence: less concerned with whether machines will become conscious than with where intelligence as a process is heading. The aptness of the dance of Shiva lies in treating creation and destruction as two aspects of one motion — and the evolution of intelligence has precisely this double character.
