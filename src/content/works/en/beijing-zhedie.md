A novella by Hao Jingfang published in 2012, translated by Ken Liu and winner of the 2016 Hugo Award for Best Novelette — the second Chinese work so honoured after the Three-Body trilogy.

A future Beijing is folded by space and time into three mutually inaccessible worlds: the first space, of five million people, has a full twenty-four hours; the second holds twenty-five million; the third, fifty million, wakes only at night and does the work of waste disposal. Lao Dao, a waste worker, risks a crossing of the three spaces to pay for his adopted daughter's schooling.

Its imagination transposes stratification into an allocation of space and time: folding is at once a physical operation and an exact metaphor for social structure. The transitions are rendered concretely, so that abstract inequality becomes visible mechanical motion.

Treating a severe subject in a gentle register, it is among the clearest social metaphors in Chinese science fiction. Filed as SF.

What distinguishes the novella is its manner of handling. Hao does not set the three spaces in a simple opposition of good and evil: those in the first space are equally bound by rules, and Lao Dao's crossing is not an act of resistance. What she depicts is an order that has already stabilised, in which everyone keeps to his place — and the sorrow lies precisely there. This temperate yet uncompromising tone sets it apart among dystopian fiction.
