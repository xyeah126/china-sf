God Is With Us is a novel Wang Jinkang published in 2011, the work he has called the true starting point of his long-form writing and the representative piece of his later philosophical science fiction. Two prologues, one mythic and one contemporary, fold a hundred-thousand-year time scale into a present-day conflict of civilizations.

In the premise, a non-human "God" from the star Engo — a guardian resembling a five-armed octopus — has watched over Earth for a hundred thousand years, witnessing humanity from its African origins to modern civilization. When the mother civilization invades Earth to expand, the lonely guardian must choose between kin and human "children." The surface plot of humanity forming a global ruling council to resist invasion is shadowed by the inner drama of the God's wavering allegiance, pressing toward questions of civilization, morality and survival.

The novel advances its well-known "symbiotic circle theory": good and evil share a common root in the survival instincts of life, and altruistic good slowly expands through a long struggle against evil, forming ever-widening circles of symbiosis. The idea is often set against the "dark forest law" of The Three-Body Problem — one speaks of enmity between civilizations, the other of how good can grow on the dung-heap of evil.

A war story on the surface and an interrogation of human nature at heart, it is Wang Jinkang's most ambitious novel in thought. Filed as SF.

Wang has explained that the "God" in the title means both the alien guardian and the objective law of nature; "us" means humanity as a whole and each individual. To write the fate of civilizations as something to be understood rather than something awaiting rescue is the consistent stance of the man sometimes called "the thinker of Chinese science fiction."
