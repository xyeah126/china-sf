Cheng Jingbo's signature work, published around 2009 and winner of Galaxy and Nebula Awards. It builds a heterogeneous world on the central image of an inverted sky.

The story is set where the relation of sky and earth is reversed, or joined in some unusual way. Its people live, move and search there, and their everyday experience is wholly altered by the spatial premise.

Its imagination belongs to a poetics of space: rather than pursuing consistency of technical premise, it takes one strong and exact image as a fulcrum and extrapolates from it a whole matching way of life. Cheng's language is delicate and her imagery singular, highly recognisable in Chinese science fiction.

The work concentrates her style. Filed as SF.

Cheng's short fiction wins its effects less through plot than through establishing a self-sufficient sense of a world. In The Inverted Sky a reader would be hard pressed to recount what happens, yet remembers clearly what the world is like. That capacity is rare in Chinese science fiction and places her work near the border of fantasy and SF — a position she does not dispute, holding classification to be of little importance.
