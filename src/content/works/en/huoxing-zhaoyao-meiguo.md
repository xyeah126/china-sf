Mars Shines on America is a full-length SF novel by Han Song, first published in 2000 as 2066: Journey to the West (Heilongjiang People's Publishing House) and revised in 2012 under its present title. Set in 2066, the book inverts the Sino-American order: China has become the world's leading power, the United States has fallen into decline and self-isolation, and global life runs on the virtual network of the super-computer "Armando". The sixteen-year-old Chinese Go prodigy Tang Long travels to New York for a world tournament and is swept into the collapse of Armando, the destruction of the World Trade Centre, the search for a prophetic "Crook-Staff" and a second American civil war — from which he is finally rescued by Chinese forces.

Han Song's trademark documentary tone — modelled on Edgar Snow's Red Star Over China and lit through Buddhist cyclical and mirror imagery — turns a dark mirror on the Chinese modernisation project. The novel's depiction of a demolished Manhattan famously predates the September 2001 attacks: drafting began in 1996, six years before the catastrophe it appears to predict. Filed as SF.
