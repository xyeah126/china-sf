A representative short story by Xiao Jianheng published around 1979, built on scientific hypotheses about dreams and consciousness. In Chinese science fiction of the late 1970s, work taking psychology and neuroscience as its subject was rare.

The story imagines recording, intervening in and even sharing dreams by technical means, raising questions about the limits of consciousness and about privacy. It advances within a popular-science frame, keeping the plainness customary to Xiao.

Its imagination opens up inner space. Unlike the period's dominant space and ocean themes, Dream turns exploration toward consciousness itself, anticipating later cyberpunk attention to mental space.

An example of the pluralism of the revival years, showing range in subject matter. Filed as SF.

Dreams were an unusual subject for Chinese science fiction in the late 1970s. The dominant imaginative spaces — outer space, the sea, the poles — all pointed outward; Xiao turned the direction of exploration inward instead. That turn recurs in later Chinese science fiction and became a routine path for cyberpunk from the 1990s onward. Dream is, in this respect, a notably early attempt.
