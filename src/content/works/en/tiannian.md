A novel published by He Xi in 2015, among the largest of his works. It sets the survival of civilisation against cyclical catastrophe on a cosmic scale.

Earth's civilisation repeatedly meets a periodic disaster determined by the mechanics of celestial motion, its period far exceeding any individual lifespan. Humanity must recognise the pattern within limited historical memory and prepare for the next occurrence.

Its imagination lies in its use of time scale: the cosmic year binds the fate of civilisation to a celestial rhythm, making the end a calculable object rather than a sudden catastrophe. From this the novel extrapolates the strategies a civilisation might adopt under cyclical blows.

Binding astronomical premise to human fate, it is He Xi's most important later novel. Filed as SF.

The cosmic year binds the lifespan of civilisation to a celestial rhythm, making the end a periodic and foreseeable event. He Xi thereby avoids the suddenness of ordinary disaster narrative and examines a long-term uncertainty instead: given that the term is fixed, does anything civilisation does still signify? The novel offers no cheap answer, letting characters of different generations respond each in his own way.
