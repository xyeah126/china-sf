He Xi's most celebrated short story, published in 2003 and long among readers' favourites. It takes a mathematician as its protagonist and is among the most moving pieces in Chinese science fiction.

A mathematician spends his life working out an entirely new theory that is too far ahead of its time to be understood; he dies alone. The theory is rediscovered a hundred years later and becomes the basis of a later breakthrough.

Its imagination lies not in technology but in time: being ahead of one's age is written as a tragic structure, in which the discoverer's worth can only be confirmed by a future he will not see. Mathematics is at once concrete content and a figure for the beauty of pure intellect.

Its emotional force has moved countless readers; it is a classic of Chinese science fiction. Filed as SF.

The story also implies a judgement about the scientific community: the worth of a theory is decided not by contemporaries but by time. He Xi does not write the indifference of the mathematician's generation as malice — they simply cannot understand, which is the colder reality. Being ahead of one's age thus ceases to be a matter of temperament and becomes a matter of timing: some are born too early, and that is no one's fault.
