A novel by Zhang Ran published around 2014, set against a global food crisis. It joins disaster narrative to reflection on the modern way of life.

A new fungus destroys the principal food crops, and global supply collapses within a short time. Social order, international relations and individual morality are all deformed under the pressure.

Its imagination lies in pushing food, the most everyday of things, to the extreme of scarcity, thereby exposing the fragile basis of modern civilisation. The realist manner gives the disaster a strong sense of actuality.

Representative of Zhang's treatment of social questions and strongly readable. Filed as SF.

Zhang handles catastrophe with restraint: no large-scale spectacle, but a collapse broken down into details of daily life — food substitutes, how trade is conducted, the shifting tacit understandings between people. The disaster becomes credible and, for that reason, more unsettling. Together with When the Sun Falls it belongs to his series on social questions, showing his continuing interest in realist material.

Now that global catastrophe has become a common subject, this restraint shows greater force than spectacle would.
