Published in the seventh issue of Science Fiction World in 2000, this is among Liu Cixin's most important novellas and one of the most discussed texts in Chinese science fiction.

The sun is about to undergo a helium flash. Humanity fits the entire planet with tens of thousands of engines and sets out with its home on a journey of twenty-five centuries and a hundred generations. The story focuses on one crisis and one revolt along the way.

The core of its imagination is taking the Earth along rather than abandoning it. This stands in sharp contrast to the spacecraft emigration common in Western science fiction, and is often read as a projection of the Chinese attachment to land, as well as a mark of Chinese SF's cultural self-awareness.

The work also carries a vast time scale and an austere collectivist logic. The 2019 film of the same name opened the era of Chinese science fiction cinema. Filed as SF.

A further much-discussed dimension is its collectivist logic: in the course of the flight individual claims must yield to the survival of the whole, and the work expresses no hesitation about this. That has prompted discussion among readers abroad about the ranking of values. Whatever a reader's position, the question the story poses — when survival is the sole end, what may be sacrificed — remains a live one.
