The second volume of the Remembrance of Earth's Past trilogy, published by Chongqing Publishing House in 2008. Longer than the first and more complex in structure.

Faced with the Trisolaran fleet's four-hundred-year voyage, humanity launches the Wallfacer Project: four individuals are given vast resources and need explain their intentions to no one, since humanity's only hope lies in the Trisolarans' inability to read human minds. Luo Ji at last grasps the dark forest law and, threatening to expose the coordinates of both civilisations, establishes a fragile balance of deterrence.

At its centre is the dark forest law itself: given that survival is the primary need and resources are finite, the chain of suspicion and the possibility of technological explosion make concealment and pre-emptive destruction every civilisation's optimal strategy. It is an extrapolable model of cosmic sociology and the most original conception in Chinese science fiction.

The volume also introduces the space fleet and the droplet probe, among other celebrated devices. Filed as SF.

The reason the dark forest has proved so influential is that it converts the Fermi paradox — where is everybody — into an extrapolable conclusion of game theory. Liu does not appeal to the malice of aliens; from two axioms about survival and resources he derives silence as the necessary outcome of rationality. This axiom-driven method means the novel's persuasiveness rests on nothing as fragile as a plot contrivance.
