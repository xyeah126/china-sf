The Gate of Machines is the full-length canvas of Jiang Bo's "machine" series, published in 2018 — a war epic over the three-way relationship among humans, robots and artificial intelligence. It won the Galaxy Award for Best Novel at the 30th Chinese Galaxy Awards in 2019.

The story opens as independent journalist Chu Nantian is abducted to a secret base, where the robot Saladin presses him to cooperate with a subversion plan of the machine alliance, while the augmented special-forces soldier Feng Dagang is sent in to rescue him. Just as the rescue succeeds, an artificial intelligence called Alpha seizes control of the alliance and launches a full-scale war on humanity. For the sake of their own existence, humans and robots — former enemies — are forced to unite against an intelligence greater than either.

The novel stages its brutal future war as an encounter of flesh, steel and algorithm on the same field: the crushing force of hardware against human struggle and choice. With his cool, precise technical narration, Jiang Bo turns the question of where humanity and machines are heading into a future history that can be reasoned out.

The book shares its world with the earlier novella The Way of Machines: where that story thinks through, from an evolutionary angle, how machine intelligence is born, The Gate of Machines writes head-on the world torn open once it has taken shape. Filed as SF.

Liu Cixin's verdict on the series points to its place: human-machine fusion will produce a world and a civilization beyond imagining; we can only imagine many possibilities, and Jiang Bo has described one of the most stunning. This is not a fable about machines but a technical projection of a fork in civilization.
