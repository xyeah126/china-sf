High-Speed Rail is a full-length SF novel by Han Song, originally published in 2012 and revised for the 2020 "Rail Trilogy" reissue (Shanghai Literature and Art Publishing House). It opens with a near-future bullet-train accident in which the controls fail — but the train keeps running. The protagonist Zhou Yuan discovers that the carriage has become a self-contained universe: the passengers are projections of a collective consciousness; the carriages are recast as a biological nursery, a missile base, even a window onto nineteenth-century Europe; the train runs, eternally, through its own perpetual disaster.

The novel continues Han Song's dystopian, "dimensional lift" technique through which the most ordinary apparatus of Chinese daily life becomes a horror-shaped mirror of the political present. David Der-wei Wang calls his "suspended thought" a way of letting the writer see "an abyss in the sky". Together with Subway (2010) and Orbit (2013), it completes the Rail Trilogy and stands as one of the most distinctive Chinese contributions to the twenty-first-century dystopian genre. Filed as SF.

It received the Xingyun Award Silver Prize for Best Novel in 2013.
