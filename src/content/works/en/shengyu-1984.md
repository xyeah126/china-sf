A novel by Hao Jingfang published in 2018, on a generation's coming of age and choices at a turning point in history. It is the most grounded of her books, with science fictional elements receding far into the background.

Two women born in 1984 are followed across decades as they choose among education, work, marriage and ideals, and as the history of their parents' generation leaves its mark on them.

Its imagination lies less in technological premise than in the systematic conception of alternative lives: the characters are repeatedly brought to forking paths, and the cost of each choice is shown. What is science-fictional here is close to a structured extrapolation of possible worlds.

The novel shows Hao's movement between literary fiction and science fiction. Filed as SF, as social science fiction in a broad sense.

The science-fictional quality seems thin here because Hao has exchanged technology for society as the object of extrapolation: what she examines is a generation's room for choice amid institutional change. The method raises a possibility — that science fiction need not require novel apparatus, and remains within the genre so long as it offers a systematic conception of possible lives. Exploration of that boundary has value in itself.
