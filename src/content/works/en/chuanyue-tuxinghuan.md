Crossing Saturn's Rings is a novel Xie Yunning published in 2020 — a survival story in space with the temper of a classical adventure. In 2021 it won both the gold prize for best novel at the 12th Xingyun Awards and the Galaxy Award for Best Novel at the 32nd Galaxy Awards, restoring the prize to a winner after a year in which it had been left vacant.

The story opens with an accident: the playboy Lu Jianli boards a ship to chase a comet, is ejected into open space, and becomes an unknown "human asteroid" adrift in the rings of Saturn. Betrayed by his circle, unreachable by rescue, out of supplies, he seems doomed — until Doris, the chief intelligence of the Saturn probe, extends a hand. He lands on Enceladus, engineers gene-edited fish that can live in its frozen sea, and clings to life in an alien ocean by almost primitive means while awaiting a rescue that must cross a hundred thousand kilometres.

The narration is carried by dense, accurate detail from astronomy and bioengineering: the particle environment of Saturn's rings, Enceladus's subsurface ocean, CRISPR gene editing are gears of the plot rather than scenery. Xie calls himself a deliberately hard writer, and this novel is the concentrated proof — the technological situation comes first, and characters and fate grow out of it.

Beneath its survival surface the novel also takes up the identity of clones, the ethics of gene editing and the emotions of artificial intelligence. The jury praised it for "using a realist perspective to approach the edge of imagination." Filed as SF.

Lu Jianli's drift is a fine layered metaphor: a man forgotten by humanity is granted, precisely by that, the distance to see humanity itself. The author does not turn him into a heroic survivor but lets him learn, piece by piece, to live with solitude and failure — the reduction of cosmic scale to personal scale is the softest part of this hard science fiction.
