Soul of the Machines, published in 2020, is Jiang Bo's sequel to the Galaxy Award-winning The Gate of Machines and the closing volume of his "machine" series. It carries the war canvas of its predecessor forward, advancing the question from "can machines replace humans" to "do machines have souls".

The story opens in the aftermath of the Alpha War: humanity has briefly won back control of the future, when Feng Hanjie — son of the war hero Feng Dagang — is brought to the Chuanjiang base and the resurrected robot Saladin storms in with its shock troops, killing Amelita, keeper of the brain-bank. Feng Hanjie sets out on a path of vengeance; meanwhile Chu Nantian senses that the brain-bank — a super-intelligence formed by the linking of countless human minds — is slipping out of control. Forces from every side converge on the north to assault it, and Feng Hanjie himself seems to be turning, step by step, into a super-intelligence like Alpha before him.

Where the earlier novel pitched humans against machines, this one shifts the battlefield to humans against a super-intelligence: a three-way struggle among humankind, the robot alliance and the runaway brain-bank, with mind-uploading, androids and nanomachines all taking the stage. Jiang Bo carries the epic in antithetical chapter headings, and Han Song likened the book to a digital-age Investiture of the Gods and Classic of Mountains and Seas — amid the carnage it keeps asking: when countless minds are bound into an existence near godhood, does it still have a "soul", and how is the individual's sorrow before the infinite and the unknown to be placed?

Together with The Way of Machines and The Gate of Machines, this novel completes a full world-view projection — where The Gate of Machines pictured one of the most stunning possibilities of a civilizational fork, Soul of the Machines stands at the end of that fork and looks back at what remains when flesh, steel and algorithm have finished entangling.
