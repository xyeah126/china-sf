A short story by He Xi published around 2005, on parting under technological intervention. Its title takes up the Buddhist suffering of separation from those one loves.

It is set in an age when the pain of parting can be managed by technical means. Yet when farewell can be cushioned, deferred or partly erased, whether parting still takes place becomes the question.

Its imagination follows He Xi's usual path: a clear technological proposition used as a lever for questions of feeling and ethics. Its edge lies in exposing the paradox of the technology — the means of relieving suffering often abolish the thing the suffering pointed to.

Brief and restrained, among his best short pieces. Filed as SF.

He Xi often titles his short fiction with Buddhist terms, and Parting is one such case. He does not treat these concepts as objects of belief but as ready-made classifications of the human situation — birth, age, sickness, death, meeting with what one hates, parting from what one loves — each pointing to a predicament that technique handles poorly. This gives his work, beneath its hard-SF shell, a pronounced tragic sense: technology can alter the conditions of a predicament, but not the predicament itself.
