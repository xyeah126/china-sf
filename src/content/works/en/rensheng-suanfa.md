A short-story collection by Chen Qiufan published in 2019, on how algorithms, data and artificial intelligence rewrite the human condition.

The pieces cover several near-future situations: algorithms deciding emotional matching, data profiles substituting for judgements of character, disputes over identity once consciousness can be copied. Each starts from an identifiable present-day technology and extrapolates a few years or decades forward.

Its imagination is near-future realism: rather than staging distant marvels, it takes technological trends already under way and pushes them to their logical next step. The anxiety thereby becomes concrete and pressing.

A strand of technological realism in Chinese science fiction, and evidence of Chen's sustained attention to artificial intelligence. Filed as SF.

Throughout the collection Chen returns to one proposition: when decisions are delegated to algorithms, do people gain freedom or give up the right to explain themselves? He does not reject technology outright but points out that every act of outsourcing carries with it a surrender of self-understanding. This stance distinguishes his work from the usual anti-technological narrative and brings it closer to a concrete description of the contemporary condition.
